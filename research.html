About the lab

Connecting immunity, inflammation, and the brain

The Zhu Lab investigates the molecular and inflammatory mechanisms underlying neuropsychiatric and neurocognitive disorders, with a focus on developing novel therapeutic strategies. Led by Dr. Xiaolei Zhu, our work integrates neuroimmunology, metabolism, and translational animal models to define how immune and metabolic signaling contribute to brain dysfunction — particularly in people living with HIV.

We have demonstrated that microglial glutaminase inhibition normalizes stress-induced behavioral abnormalities, that nSMase2 inhibition improves neurobehavioral deficits in HIV models, and that gut immune signaling via Dectin-1 plays a critical role in psychosocial stress responses — work featured on the cover of Nature Immunology in 2023 and covered by over 150 media outlets worldwide.

Beyond HIV, we examine how chronic stress and adolescent cannabis exposure dysregulate neuroimmune circuits underlying mood and cognition. Dr. Zhu holds active NIH funding as sole PI from both NIMH and NHLBI.

Stress-induced Psychosocial Behavior

Identifying how stress-associated molecular pathways — including gut immune signaling and microglial glutaminase activity — drive depression-relevant behaviors, and developing novel therapeutic interventions.

HIV & CNS Comorbidities

Understanding the pathogenesis of cognitive dysfunction, depression, and sleep deficits in people living with HIV to identify novel therapeutic interventions, including glutaminase inhibitors and nSMase2 inhibition.

Genetic Factors & Neurodevelopment

Examining how genetic predisposition and environmental exposures — including adolescent cannabis use and developmental stress — synergistically impair cognition and neurodevelopment via inflammatory signaling.

Complementary & Integrative Medicine

Investigating traditional Chinese medicine-based compounds and integrative approaches as sources of novel diagnostics and therapeutic agents for neuropsychiatric and inflammatory conditions.

Current projects

Active research programmes

NIH/NHLBI R01 · 2025–2029
PI: Dr. Xiaolei Zhu

The role of microglial glutaminase in HIV-induced sleep deficits

Sleep disturbances are among the most prevalent and debilitating CNS co-morbidities in virologically suppressed people living with HIV, yet their mechanisms remain poorly understood and there are no targeted treatments. This project aims to elucidate the role of microglial glutaminase in HIV-induced sleep deficits and assess whether its inhibition can reverse the sleep deficits and associated microglia molecular alterations in two established murine NeuroHIV models — the EcoHIV and proBLT mouse models.

We will evaluate the efficacy of a novel dendrimer-glutaminase inhibitor conjugate (D-TTM020) that selectively targets activated microglia, leveraging expertise in neuroinflammation, HIV preclinical models, drug discovery, and sleep medicine. These studies could lead to the development of innovative therapeutics to prevent or reverse HIV-induced sleep disturbances in the growing population of virologically suppressed people living with HIV.

NIH/NIMH R56 · 2024–2027
PI: Dr. Xiaolei Zhu

Microglia-targeted glutaminase inhibition for the treatment of chronic stress-associated depression

Major depressive disorder affects hundreds of millions of people worldwide, yet current antidepressants fail a substantial proportion of patients. Building on our prior work demonstrating that glutaminase inhibitor JHU-083 blocks upregulated microglial glutaminase activity and normalizes stress-induced psychosocial behavioral abnormalities, this project develops a next-generation approach using a hydroxyl-dendrimer nanoparticle delivery system to target the inhibitor directly to activated microglia.

We will evaluate the efficacy and safety of dendrimer-glutaminase inhibitor conjugates in established chronic social defeat stress and chronic unpredictable stress mouse models. This work establishes microglial metabolic signaling as a therapeutic target for depression and advances a novel, selective drug delivery strategy with strong translational potential.
